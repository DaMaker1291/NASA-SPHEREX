HOW TO SPOT MOTION

A tiny movie of a very large sky.

SPHEREx launched in March 2025 and revisits the same strip of sky across its survey. Its DR1 archive therefore starts with the 2025 survey; there is no earlier SPHEREx data. Differences between visits can reveal proper motion, variable stars, or an object that moved between exposures.

Blink or subtract

Blink A / B toggles two visits. Difference subtracts them so changes in position or brightness become obvious.

The bundled mirror is a small real-data sample per detector, not the complete SPHEREx archive. The date window in the viewer belongs only to the visits actually loaded; a live archive query can return a different window.

LESSON 01

What is a SPHEREx visit?

Each visit is a real level-2 spectral image. The detector records many narrow wavelength channels at once; the cutout shows a spatial slice of that exposure.

LESSON 02

Why change bands?

D1 to D6 cover different infrared windows. A star, dust cloud or brown dwarf can look completely different from one band to the next because different physical processes glow at different wavelengths.

LESSON 03

Why blink visits?

The same patch is observed at different times. Blinking two visits makes proper motion, variable stars and transient brightness changes easier to see than any single frame.
